Biography

A scholar who never left the street

Wayne Martin Mellinger, Ph.D., is an independent sociologist and public intellectual based in Santa Barbara, California, working at the intersection of ethnomethodology, institutional analysis, and the sociology of homelessness. He trained at the University of California, Santa Barbara under Don H. Zimmerman, Thomas P. Wilson, and Harvey Molotch, in a department that helped found the ethnomethodology and conversation analysis tradition — the close, empirical study of how social order is accomplished in the fine grain of everyday interaction.

For nearly two decades, that training has been tested against the street. As a street outreach worker in Santa Barbara County, Wayne has sat across from people navigating intake interviews, waitlists, discharge planning, and the countless small documentary transactions through which institutions decide who is seen and who is not. That vantage point is the empirical ground for his current theoretical project, Critical Interactionism: an approach that treats abandonment not as an absence of resources or compassion, but as something organizations actively accomplish, in real time, through the ordinary mechanics of paperwork, categorization, and deferral.

He serves on the Santa Barbara County Behavioral Wellness Commission and the Santa Barbara County Continuum of Care, and sits on the boards of several nonprofits working on homelessness and social justice, including CLUE-SB, Showers of Blessing, and the Committee for Social Justice. Since 2012, he has written more than a hundred columns for the online newspaper Noozhawk, translating research and street-level observation for a general audience.

Wayne's scholarship extends into two ongoing research platforms. Doing Modernity: Studies in Critical Interactionism houses his work on institutions, recognition, and the Santa Barbara School of ethnomethodology. The Dionysian Naturalist: Studies in Religious Naturalism and Other Emerging Nature Religions develops a parallel line of inquiry into contemporary Paganism, religious naturalism, and ecstatic spiritual practice — a personal theological framework he calls Dionysian Naturalism.

Each December 21st, Wayne organizes the Longest Night Homeless Memorial Vigil at the Santa Barbara County Courthouse, a ritual of public mourning for community members who died while homeless that year. He co-founded the Santa Barbara Homeless Foot Washing and the Freedom Warming Centers. He is a practicing member of the Unitarian Society of Santa Barbara, and also writes poetry and literary fiction alongside his scholarly and journalistic work.

The thread running through all of it: rigorous sociological method, a theology of nature and embodiment, and a refusal to separate scholarship from the work of showing up.

Religion & Philosophy

Dionysian Naturalism

Alongside his sociology, Wayne writes and develops theology under The Dionysian Naturalist: Studies in Religious Naturalism and Other Emerging Nature Religions — a personal framework, Dionysian Naturalism, sitting at the crossing of Religious Naturalism, contemporary Paganism, and the human sciences.

His interests here range across Western esoteric traditions, entheogenic spirituality, Druidry, and nature-based ritual practice, and draw on figures such as Abraham Joshua Heschel, Robin Wall Kimmerer, Annie Dillard, and Mary Oliver. He is a practicing member of the Unitarian Society of Santa Barbara.

A theology built for people who take both ecology and ecstasy seriously.

Poetry

Not everything here needs a citation.

I have knelt on sidewalks
that never asked to hold a body,
and learned that attention, given plainly,
is its own kind of shelter.
The courthouse steps in December
do not warm anyone.
We light candles there anyway,
because naming is a form of care
the paperwork forgot.
Somewhere between the file and the face
a person goes missing.
I have spent my life trying
to notice exactly where.
From an ongoing collection — Moments of Awe Punctuated by the Mundane
